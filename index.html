I'm Andy Aketch, a Geomatic Engineering graduate based in Nairobi, Kenya, working at the intersection of spatial data science, remote sensing, and software development.

I build end-to-end geospatial pipelines — from processing satellite imagery in Google Earth Engine and running machine learning models in Python, to designing spatial databases in PostGIS and delivering results through interactive web maps.

My work spans land cover change analysis, ecological quality assessment, network accessibility modelling, and field survey, with a focus on translating complex spatial data into outputs that are clear and actionable. I'm currently expanding into full-stack development to bridge the gap between geospatial analysis and the web applications that make it accessible.

PostGIS Spatial SQL Accessibility Modeling

ASAL Healthcare Accessibility

A PostGIS-based geospatial database and analysis project examining healthcare accessibility across Kenya's Arid and Semi-Arid Land (ASAL) counties. It brings together spatial data on health facilities and population distribution to identify gaps in access to care across these underserved regions, using PostGIS for spatial queries and analysis.

Remote Sensing Machine Learning SHAP Interpretability RSEI & LULC

Kiambu County Land Use & Environment Study

A remote sensing and machine learning study of environmental change in Kiambu County, Kenya. It combines RSEI (Remote Sensing Ecological Index) and LULC (Land Use/Land Cover) classification with SHAP analysis to interpret which factors are driving the changes detected in satellite imagery. The findings were written up as a Medium blog series, translating the technical analysis into a narrative on how the county's land and environment are shifting over time.
